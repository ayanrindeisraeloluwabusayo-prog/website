# 3D body explainer (study of five reference shorts, technique only)

Studied frame by frame (2 frames a second) on 2026-11-12. Technique only: never reuse the source footage,
characters, wording or branding.

## What the style does
- **A person acts out the sentence.** A 3D human does the thing the voice says (lifts a bottle, puts gum in the
  mouth, runs, walks to a corner). The viewer watches a person, not a diagram.
- **One continuous camera.** The camera rarely cuts. It pushes in from a waist-up shot to the face, to the
  mouth, then *through the skin* into the body, and follows the thing (a drop, a molecule, a piece of gum).
- **Skin turns to glass.** At the moment of entry the skin goes see-through blue with bright rim light, and
  the organs inside keep full colour (red vessels, pink brain, green stomach acid).
- **The "thing" is a hero object.** Small, bright, simple (glowing dots for molecules, a pink block of gum),
  always centre frame, and the camera travels with it.
- **Cause and effect on screen.** Each step is shown happening: dots leave the vessel, dock on receptors,
  a bar fills up; the gum shrinks in acid; a brain appears above a tired runner.
- **Simple worlds.** Plain sky-blue studio with a faint grid for body shots; one tidy room (classroom, running
  track) for behaviour shots. Saturated, clean, toy-like colours.
- **Small on-screen helpers.** Highlight rings on the floor, swirl lines for smell, angle labels, arrows,
  a formula card. Used sparingly, in one accent colour.
- **Captions.** One to three words, white with a dark outline, centre of the lower-middle screen, changing
  with the voice.
- **Pace.** A new action or camera stage about every 1.5-2.5 s; 24-47 s long; a question or "what if" first.

## How it joins the owner's blue explainer edit
Keep: the question hook, Brian's voice, serif key words, studies on paper, original music, quiet effects.
Add: one recurring 3D person who acts out each line, and "fly inside the body" shots with glass skin for the
mechanism. Real pictures become occasional, not the backbone.
