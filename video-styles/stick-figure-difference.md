# Stick-figure "what's the difference?" explainer

Frame-by-frame study of three reference videos the owner sent on 15 Nov 2026 (drugs, psychology terms, film terms;
67-82 s each, 720x1280, 30 fps). Technique only: never reuse the creator's stick man drawing, handle, watermark,
wording or pictures. Draw our own character and use licensed pictures.

## The format (identical in all three)

One video = 5 or 6 pairs of things people mix up. Every pair runs the same four beats, 11-16 s per pair:

| Beat | Voice | Caption | Stick man | Pictures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "This is A." | `this is` + **A in a colour** | points up-left, at picture A, cocky smirk | A's label and picture appear, left |
| 2 | "This is B." | `this is` + **B in another colour** | mirrored: points up-right at picture B | B's label and picture appear, right |
| 3 | "What's the difference?" | `what's the difference ?` (all black) | shrug, both palms up, raised eyebrow, a small `?` above the head | both stay |
| 4a | "A is ... (one clause)" | black, 2-3 words at a time | points up-left again, mouth now talking | both stay |
| 4b | "while B is ... (one clause)" | black, 2-3 words at a time | flips to point up-right on the word "while" | both stay |

Then a hard cut to the next pair: everything is replaced at once. No intro, no title card, no hook line: the first
frame is already "This is A". Last 3-4 s: the final frame dims to about 30 % and the creator's logo and handle sit
in the middle.

## Layout (720x1280 frame, pure white background)

- Top 6 %: empty (a faint watermark sits here in the reference).
- Labels at ~9 % down: A's name over the left picture, B's name over the right. Black, bold, small (about 3 % of
  frame height).
- Pictures at 11-25 %: two squares side by side, each ~43 % of the width, small gap, no border, no rounded corners,
  no shadow. Real photos or film stills cropped tight on the subject (a face showing the feeling, or the object on
  white). The picture is evidence, not decoration: it shows the thing being named.
- Caption at ~27.5 %: one centred line, directly under the pictures and above the head.
- Stick man at 30-50 % of the height, centred, about 35 % of the frame wide.
- Bottom half of the frame: empty white. (Leaves room for the app's own buttons and captions.)

## The stick man

- Thick black marker lines with slightly uneven, hand-drawn edges. Round head (white fill), 2-3 spiky hairs on top,
  dot eyes with slanted eyebrows, one-line mouth, a tiny tie or lanyard at the neck, line body, long straight legs
  apart, small oval feet. Hands are drawn properly: a pointing finger on one hand, an open palm on the other.
- Small "emphasis ticks" (2-3 short strokes) next to each hand, and a `?` over the head for the shrug.
- **He does not move between poses.** Checked frame by frame: every change is a hard cut from one drawing to the
  next in a single frame, with no in-between, no slide and no bounce. He is a set of still drawings.
- Pose set (about 6 drawings in total):
  1. point up-left, smirk (beat 1)
  2. point up-right, smirk (beat 2), the mirror image
  3. shrug with `?` (beat 3)
  4. point up-left, explaining: 2-3 mouth shapes (smile, open, wide open) that swap each time the caption changes,
     so he looks like he is talking (beat 4a)
  5. the same, mirrored to point up-right (beat 4b)
- Legs and feet never change; only arms, face and mouth do.

## The words on screen

- Font: a very heavy, rounded cartoon display face (the Lilita One / Luckiest Guy family of looks), with a thin
  white outline so it stays readable. Labels above the pictures use the same heavy black style, a little wider.
- All lowercase in captions except names and abbreviations (PTSD, OCD, LSD).
- Colour rule: captions are black. **Only the name of the thing is coloured, and only in beats 1 and 2**
  (`this is PTSD`): A gets one bright colour, B a different one (orange, green, blue, pink, purple; a new pair of
  colours for each pair). In beat 4 everything is black, even the names.
- The caption changes in hard cuts, 2-3 words at a time, in step with the voice. No word-by-word pop, no fade,
  no highlight sweep. Phrases are cut on meaning ("is triggered" / "by a single" / "traumatic event").

## Timing and cuts

- 30 fps. Every change (picture B arriving, caption, pose) lands on the same frame as the voice starts the phrase.
- Beat lengths in the psychology video: "This is A" ~1.6 s, "This is B" ~1.7 s, "What's the difference?" ~0.9 s,
  explanation ~7 s (about 3 s for A, 4 s for B).
- No transitions anywhere: no zoom, no shake, no flash, no camera move. The only softness is a ~10-frame fade-in on
  the very first frame of the video.

## Voice and sound

- One calm, clear narrator, 2.6-3.0 words a second (slower than our story edits at 3.2-3.8), short sentences.
- Script per pair, about 30-40 words: "This is A. This is B. What's the difference? A is [what it is] that
  [what it does], while B is [what it is] that [what it does]."
- Measured: the pauses between sentences are almost silent, so there is no music bed loud enough to notice, and a
  few pauses carry a short sound (a pop or click on a change). The voice does nearly all the work.

## Why it works

- The question "what's the difference?" is the hook, repeated every 12 s, so there are 5-6 fresh hooks per video.
- Pairs people really mix up: the viewer checks themselves each time and stays for the next pair.
- Zero motion design to follow: the eye only moves between picture, one caption line and the character's face.
- The character gives the page a face and makes every video instantly recognisable.

## To make our own version

- Our own stick character (different head, hair and details), drawn as SVG poses: point-left, point-right, shrug,
  talk-left x3 mouths, talk-right x3 mouths.
- Free rounded display font (for example Lilita One or Baloo 2, both open licence).
- Licensed pictures only (Pixabay, public domain), never film stills or celebrity faces.
- Free voice (Kokoro) at about 2.8 words a second; captions cut every 2-3 words from the word timings.
- End card: the owner's own page (Human Nature Explained for psychology topics).
