# The owner's named script styles

The owner picks a style by name ("use You First", "use Star Proof", "use Full Blend"). Every fact in every style must be real and
checked before the script is shown; nothing is made up. Prices and money are in US dollars (most viewers are
outside Nigeria): never naira.

## You First
The style of the doorway, call-of-the-void and bystander videos.
1. Open by putting the viewer in the scene, in the present tense ("You're standing on a balcony.").
2. Short, clear lines, easy to follow at the slower voice (about 3.2 words a second).
3. The real study, with its real numbers.
4. "But here's the twist."
5. One strong closing line the viewer can use or repeat ("Point at one person.").

## Star Proof
Everything in You First, in the same order, plus a famous person.
1. Open on the viewer, exactly as in You First.
2. Straight after the opening: a real, checked moment or quote from a famous person (their own public words; never
   a diagnosis, never an invented quote, no politics that splits the audience).
3. The study and numbers.
4. The twist.
5. The close does two things, in this order: it goes back to the famous person, then it turns to the viewer with
   something they can do or take away, so the last line is for them ("...Michelle Obama hears it too. And next time
   someone praises your work, write it down.").

## Full Blend
All three styles in one script, each doing one job. The owner's preferred mix (first used on the impostor video).
1. **You First opens** (about 8 s): short lines that put the viewer inside the feeling.
2. **Star Proof next** (about 15 s): open it with a curious "Remember when…?" question that pulls the viewer in (the owner finds
   "Now listen to this" rude; don't use it), then the famous person's real, checked words or moment.
3. **Story Flow carries the science** (about 35 s): one flowing story, sentences joined by "now", "and you see", "so".
   The turn is always worded **"But here's the twist:"** (never "the strange part").
4. **Close:** back to the famous person, then to the viewer with something they can do ("...because Michelle Obama
   hears it too. And next time someone praises your work, write it down...").

## Story Flow
The style of the owner's five reference narrators (lyrics and journey-home videos); full rules in story-narration.md.
Opens in the middle of a story, long flowing sentences (14-20 words) joined by "then", "but", "so", "you see".
