# Stylised 3D fashion character (study of four reference stills, technique only)

Studied on 2026-11-12. Technique only: make original characters, never copy the reference characters, and
never put brand names, logos or wording on the clothes.

## What makes the look
- **Animated-film 3D, not realistic.** Faces are sculpted and simplified: clean planes, strong eyebrows,
  heavy-lidded eyes, a neat beard or sharp hairline. Skin is smooth and matte with a soft inner glow.
- **Exaggerated proportions.** Small head, long neck, very long legs, big hands, oversized shoes. One figure
  flips it: a huge barrel torso on short legs. The silhouette is the character.
- **Real-looking clothes on a cartoon body.** Chunky knit, canvas, leather and denim are rendered in fine
  detail, always oversized, pooling at the ankle. Two or three colours per outfit, one of them strong.
- **Fashion-shoot pose.** Weight on one leg, hand in pocket or arms crossed, head tilted down, three-quarter
  view, slightly low camera so the legs look longer.
- **Empty white studio.** Pure white background, soft even light, only a soft shadow under the shoes.

## Prompt recipe (image generator)
"Full-body stylized 3D animated-film character render of [who], standing on a pure white seamless studio
background with a soft contact shadow. Exaggerated proportions: [long neck, long legs, small head, big hands,
oversized shoes]. Sculpted simplified face with [features, hair]. Relaxed fashion-model pose, [pose],
three-quarter view. Oversized streetwear with believable fabric detail: [top], [trousers], [shoes]. Smooth
matte skin with soft subsurface glow, soft even studio lighting, gentle rim light, no text, no logos."

## Use in the videos
These are still pictures, not 3D models. They can be a channel host or cut-out characters that slide, tilt
and gesture a little; real acting needs an image-to-video tool.
