# Style study: the "blue serif explainer"

Studied frame by frame from three reference shorts the owner supplied (a talking-head psychology
explainer format). This file records the **technique only**: no footage, words or branding from those
videos is kept here. Use it to make the owner's own videos in this style, never to copy them.

## The format in one line
A speaker talks; every few seconds the picture cuts away to a mood image (film still, painting,
archive photo, simple blue diagram), while the spoken words build on screen one at a time, with the
one key word in each phrase set huge in a narrow serif. Electric blue and paper white tie it together.

So: a new picture roughly every 2 seconds, never longer than about 7; mixed loud and flat, like social
media expects.

## Typography (the signature)
**Two typefaces, one size jump.**
1. **Small words: a neo-grotesk sans** (Helvetica-style; free match: Inter or Arimo). Regular weight,
   white, about **4.5–5 % of the frame width** in size (≈ 32–36 px at 720 wide). Normal letter spacing.
2. **The key word: a high-contrast, narrow newspaper serif** (Times-style, squeezed to ~85 % width with
   tight letter spacing; free matches: Instrument Serif, Libre Caslon Condensed). White, about
   **3.5–4× the small size** (≈ 120–140 px). Very tight line spacing: lines touch or overlap.
   - Video 1 uses **Helvetica Bold** (heavy, tight) for the key word instead of the serif. Same rule,
     different face.
3. **The lockup:** the small words sit **above-left** of the big word, their baseline just above the big
   word's cap height, nearly touching; trailing small words sit **below, centred or right**
   ("is expressing / **hatred** / for their partner,"). Two-line big phrases stack with negative
   leading ("**motivates / social rejection**").
4. **Building word by word:** each word appears exactly as it is spoken. A word fades in from blurred
   and transparent to sharp in **3–4 frames (~0.12 s)**. Words keep their final layout positions, so a
   half-built phrase shows gaps where later words will land. When the phrase ends, the whole group
   clears and the next phrase starts fresh. The block re-centres slightly as it grows.
5. **Placement:** centred horizontally; vertically around **45–60 % of the frame** (chest height on
   the speaker; mid-frame on cut-aways and cards). Never at the bottom like normal subtitles.
6. **Colour of text:** white on pictures; **blue on white cards** ("big questions of **meaning**").
   Quotes keep their curly quotation marks in the serif.

## Colour
- **Electric/cobalt blue** cards: `#004CFF` (v1), `#0243F1` (v3), a lighter `#0A81F3` (v2), and a
  darker blue tint `#022EC6` used to wash the speaker shot in blue.
- **White / paper** cards: clean `#FBF8FC`, or textured paper `#ECEBEF` with grain.
- **Accent flash yellow** `#F3C21D` (transitions only), and a **highlighter yellow** for marking a line
  on a scanned research paper.
- Cut-away footage is graded moody: black and white, warm amber or teal-dark; often slightly darkened
  so white text reads.

## Image treatments ("the grainy look")
- **Film grain / noise** over cards and many images (noise strength about 5–8 on a 0–255 scale on flat
  colour areas); paper cards have a visible fibre and crease texture.
- **Blue monochrome (duotone):** photos and illustrations turned into blue-and-white, often with a
  crumpled-paper texture on top ("most couples" under an umbrella).
- **Stipple / sand dissolve:** a head silhouette made of grainy dots dissolving at its edges, on a blue
  gradient.
- **Simple line diagrams in white on blue:** a head outline with a fuzzy "brain" dome, dots for the
  eyes; blueprint-style brain with measurement lines; circles, dials and arcs that draw on.
- **Collage:** archive engravings, book pages, newspaper scans and portraits as flat photo "cards" with
  soft drop shadows, overlapping, on a blue radial gradient; they slide or pop in one by one.
- **Research proof:** a scanned paper on white, and the key sentence gets a **yellow highlighter wipe**
  left to right.
- **Old art and film stills** as emotional shorthand: classical paintings, sculptures, silhouettes,
  famous film moments; mostly with a slow drift or push-in so the frame is never frozen.

## Motion
- Cut-aways almost always **move slowly** (push-in, drift, or the paper/photo rotating slightly in 3D).
- Cards **pop in** from small to full size on a soft ease, or draw on (lines, arcs, circles).
- The speaker shot itself is static; punch-in by cutting to a tighter crop, never by zooming live.

## Transitions (the kit)
Most cuts are **hard cuts on the beat of speech**. About one cut in four gets a stylised transition
lasting **4–8 frames (0.13–0.27 s)**:
1. **Film burn / light leak:** the frame washes to warm pink-orange-blue light and white, with a
   film-edge strip and letters ("LENGTH", "ROLL") sliding vertically, then resolves into the next shot.
2. **Invert + colour flash:** 1–2 frames of the colour-negative image (cyan face), then 2 frames of
   solid yellow, then the blue-tinted next shot (or the reverse).
3. **Datamosh / pixel glitch:** the image smears into big blocky compression squares for ~5 frames
   before settling.
4. **Flash-frame montage:** 3–6 different images for 2 frames each (statues, faces) before landing on
   the held shot.
5. **Pop-in reveal:** on a white card, a small photo appears and scales up to full, with the next
   word landing with it.
6. **Push to extreme close-up:** a hard cut to a big eye or detail as the speaker says "in truth".

## Sound
- **A constant soft music bed** under the voice the whole time (no true silence anywhere).
- **Hits and whooshes on the stylised transitions:** loudness jumps by 8–14 dB for 0.2–0.3 s exactly on
  them.
- Voice up front; heavy compression (the loudness barely varies).

## Structure
Hook line over a striking image in the first second ("You need to be bored", "Most people assume") →
speaker → evidence (researcher, paper, study name) → metaphor images → back to the speaker for the
conclusion → a call to action card on blue ("Comment …"). The speaker is on screen about half the time.

## How to apply it to the owner's videos
- Keep the owner's **SaaS motion design** rules (see CLAUDE.md) and add this style's typography,
  word-by-word captions, blue/paper cards, grain and the transition kit on top.
- Use the owner's own footage, clips they have the rights to, public-domain art and original diagrams.
  Never reuse the reference videos' footage, speaker, wording or branding.

## Chosen fonts (tested side by side against the reference frames)
- **Key word, serif:** Newsreader 500, condensed to 88 % width, letter-spacing −0.045em, line height
  0.84, about 200 px on a 1080-wide frame. The closest free match to the reference serif; Instrument
  Serif was too thin and read as cheap.
- **Small words:** Inter Tight 400, about 51 px, letter-spacing −0.018em (the Helvetica role).
- **Statement key word on cards:** Inter Tight 800, letter-spacing −0.05em.
- **Quotes:** Newsreader 500 italic.
- **Lockup spacing:** a small lead-in line sits just above the serif word (about 7.5 % of the big size
  overlap), never touching its tall letters.
- **The standout word** is the one that carries the meaning or the surprise (a noun or verb of the
  claim: "chemistry", "peak", "dopamine", "before", "scanned", "reward"), never a filler word.
- **Placement is decided by the scene:** the text goes into the empty part of the picture (top-left
  when the subject fills the bottom, top-right when it sits lower-left, centred on open backgrounds),
  and the font style follows the scene type (serif on pictures, heavy grotesk on statement cards, italic
  serif for quotes).
- **No collisions, ever (measured, not guessed):** after the words are drawn, the real position of
  every tall letter (b d f h k l t, capitals, i/j dots) and hanging letter (g j p q y , ;) is measured.
  Small words above a big word tuck into a gap between its tall letters when one is wide enough (like
  "is expressing / hatred"), otherwise they sit clear above the tallest letter; small words below clear
  its hanging letters.
- **Font colours (owner's rule):** white on photos, footage and dark or blue backgrounds; **black on
  white or paper**; blue only as an accent (a single key word on a white card, or an occasional blue
  card). Never blue in every scene.
