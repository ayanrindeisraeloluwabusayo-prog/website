In 2006, a psychologist called Gabriel Radvansky had people carry objects from one table to another in a virtual world. Sometimes they just crossed the room. Sometimes they walked through a doorway. Same distance. Same time. But after the doorway, they forgot what they were carrying far more often. Then his team tried it with real rooms. Same result. Here's why. Your brain doesn't record your day like one long video. It cuts it into scenes, like episodes. And a doorway tells your brain: new scene. So it files the old scene away, and the reason you got up gets filed with it. But here's the twist. In 2021, another team found the doorway only wins when your head is already full. When you're juggling other thoughts, that's when the door wipes it. So next time it happens, don't stand there. Walk back through the door. The old scene comes back, and so does the reason.
