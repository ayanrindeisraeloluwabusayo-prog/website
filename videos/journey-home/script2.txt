You leave home at eight, and the drive feels like it's never going to end. Every red light, every slow car, it all drags. Then you drive back on the exact same road, and somehow you're home before you even noticed. Same road, same distance, so what changed? Most people think it's because you know the way back. So in 2011, a team in the Netherlands put that to the test. They sent people home on a completely different road, same length, and the trip home still felt shorter. So it was never about knowing the road. You see, on the way out, your brain makes a guess, say, one hour. But the trip runs longer, and your brain feels cheated. So on the way back, it braces for the worst. Long trip, it thinks, get ready. Then the drive turns out to be just normal, and compared to what you braced for, normal feels fast. Now here's the weird part. In 2015, researchers checked how long the trips felt while people were still on the road, and both trips felt about the same. The shortcut only shows up later, when you're home and you look back. Which means the trip home was never actually shorter. Your memory just tells the story that way.
