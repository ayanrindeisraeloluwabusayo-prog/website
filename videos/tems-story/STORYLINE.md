# Tems: "I thought they said I didn't have fans in Nigeria" (storyline draft 1)

Reference studied: "Singers that got rejected before they became big" (72 s, word-by-word captions in a bold sans,
fast cuts every ~1.5 s, real footage, tweet/chat screenshots, cover art, a year on screen for each step).
We keep the message (rejected, then proven right) but tell it our way: start from the Lagos moment, story-paced
narration, blue explainer look, graphics for the facts, our own music.

## Script (about 60 s)
Lagos, September. Tems is on stage in a small room, surrounded on every side by people singing her songs back to
her. And she stops. "I was confused," she says. "I thought they said I didn't have fans in Nigeria."
Here's the twist. Nobody in that room bought a ticket. Spotify invited every one of them, picked by what they
actually listen to. The proof was in the data.
Which is funny, because a few years earlier, almost nobody wanted to hear her. "No one wanted to produce for me
back then," she said. So she did it herself, hours of YouTube tutorials, learning to produce in her room.
In 2018, she quit her marketing job to release her first single, Mr. Rebel. She hadn't even finished making it yet.
It didn't blow up. Then came Try Me. Then Wizkid's Essence. Then Drake. Then a song sampling her voice went to
number one in America, and she walked away with a Grammy.
So when she says she was confused, she's right to be. The fans were always there. They were just quiet, until
someone counted them.
