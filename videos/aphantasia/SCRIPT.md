# Some people can't picture anything in their mind (Full Blend, v1, approved; thumbnail C)

Close your eyes and picture an apple. Can you see it? The red, the shine, the little stem? But some people see nothing at all. Just darkness. Remember when Ed Catmull, a co-founder of Pixar, the studio behind Toy Story, found out he couldn't picture images in his own mind? He spent a week trying to imagine a simple sphere, and saw nothing. And you see, in 2015, a neurologist at the University of Exeter, Adam Zeman, gave this a name: aphantasia, a mind without pictures. Early estimates suggest around two or three people in every hundred have it. Many don't find out until they're adults, because they thought "picture it" was just a figure of speech. In 2016, Blake Ross, one of the creators of the Firefox web browser, wrote about finding out he had it as a grown man. But here's the twist: it can change how you feel. In 2021, researchers in Sydney had people read scary stories while they measured the sweat response on their skin. Most people's bodies reacted as they imagined the scene. People with aphantasia barely reacted at all. Without the pictures, the fear never fully arrives. So if you can't see the apple, nothing is broken. Your mind just works in words and facts instead of images. Ask your friends to picture an apple tonight. You might be surprised by the answers.

## Sources (checked)
- Ed Catmull (Pixar co-founder) has described aphantasia; he tried for a week to visualise a sphere and saw nothing (his essay/interviews; reported widely, e.g. Scribd copy "Aphantasia: Ex-Pixar Chief Ed Catmull").
- Zeman, Dewar & Della Sala, Cortex (2015), University of Exeter: coined "aphantasia" (21 people with lifelong absence of visual imagery); early prevalence estimates about 2-3%.
- Blake Ross (Firefox co-creator), "Aphantasia: How It Feels To Be Blind In Your Mind", 23 April 2016.
- Wicken, Keogh & Pearson, Proceedings of the Royal Society B (2021), UNSW Sydney: skin conductance rose for most people reading/imagining frightening stories but stayed flat for people with aphantasia.
