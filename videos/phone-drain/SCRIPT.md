# Your phone drains your brain even when it's switched off (Full Blend, v1, waiting for approval)

Your phone is on the table. Screen down. Switched off. You're not even touching it. It may still be costing you. Remember when Steve Jobs was asked if his kids loved the new iPad, and he said, "They haven't used it. We limit how much technology our kids use at home"? The man who made it kept it away from his own children. And you see, there may be a reason. In 2017, Adrian Ward and his team at the University of Texas gave nearly eight hundred people tests of memory and problem-solving. Some had their phone on the desk, face down. Some had it in a pocket or bag. Some left it in another room. The people whose phones were in another room did best, and it made no difference whether the phone was on or off. The researchers think part of your brain stays busy just not checking it. But here's the twist: the people who said they depended on their phones the most were hit the hardest. And science isn't finished with this one. A careful repeat study in 2022 found no clear drain, while other studies have found it again. So the safest bet is also the easiest. Next time you need to think, don't just switch your phone off. Put it in another room.

## Sources (checked)
- Steve Jobs to Nick Bilton (late 2010), published in the New York Times, 11 Sep 2014, "Steve Jobs Was a Low-Tech Parent": "They haven't used it. We limit how much technology our kids use at home."
- Ward, Duke, Gneezy & Bos, "Brain Drain: The mere presence of one's own smartphone reduces available cognitive capacity", Journal of the Association for Consumer Research 2(2):140-154 (2017), University of Texas at Austin (McCombs): nearly 800 smartphone users; phone on desk (face down), in pocket/bag, or in another room; another room did best on working memory and fluid intelligence; on/off made no difference; effects strongest for the most phone-dependent.
- Ruiz Pardo & Minda, "Reexamining the 'brain drain' effect: A replication of Ward et al. (2017)", Acta Psychologica 230:103717 (2022): pre-registered replication, no effect of mere presence. Böttger, Poschik & Zierer (2023) review: many studies confirming the effect. So the evidence is mixed.
