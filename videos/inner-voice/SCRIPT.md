# Some people have no voice in their head (Full Blend, v1, approved; thumbnail C)

Right now, as you listen to this, is there a voice in your head, talking along? For many people, there's a running commentary all day long. But some people have nothing. No inner voice at all. Remember when that question went viral online, and people couldn't believe others didn't have one? And you see, in 2024, the psychologists Johanne Nedergaard, at the University of Copenhagen, and Gary Lupyan, at the University of Wisconsin, gave it a name: anendophasia, a life without inner speech. They compared people who say they almost never hear an inner voice with people who hear one almost constantly. The quiet group did worse at holding a list of words in memory, and was slower to tell whether the names of two pictures rhymed. But here's the twist: even for people who do have an inner voice, it's quieter than they think. When the psychologist Russell Hurlburt beeped people at random moments and asked what was in their mind right then, inner speech showed up only just over a quarter of the time. So whether your head is noisy or silent, there's no single right way to think. Some think in words, some in pictures, some in feelings. Ask the person next to you if they hear their own voice. You might be surprised.

## Sources (checked)
- Nedergaard & Lupyan, "Not everybody has an inner voice: Behavioral consequences of anendophasia", Psychological Science (2024), University of Copenhagen / University of Wisconsin-Madison: 46 people with low vs 47 with high self-reported inner speech; low group worse on verbal working memory and slower at rhyme judgments of pictured objects. Groups were based on self-report (a 2025 commentary questions whether inner speech is truly absent).
- Heavey & Hurlburt (2008), descriptive experience sampling (random beeps): inner speaking in about 28% of samples, with large individual differences (Hurlburt, Heavey & Kelsey 2013).
- The "do you have an inner monologue?" question went viral online in 2020.
