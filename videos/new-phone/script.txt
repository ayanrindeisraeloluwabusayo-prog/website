You unbox the new phone. The shine, the smell, you check it every five minutes. Three weeks later, it's just your phone again. Remember when the first iPhone came out in 2007? People lined up outside stores for hours, some for days, just to hold one. The phone in your pocket today does far more, and you barely notice it. Now, psychologists call this hedonic adaptation, and in 1978 Philip Brickman and his team went looking for some of the luckiest people they could find: twenty-two big lottery winners. And you see, when they compared them with ordinary people, the winners were not happier, and they actually took less pleasure from everyday things, like hearing a funny joke or getting a compliment. Your brain gets used to good things, so the thrill fades. But here's the twist: a break can bring it back. In 2009, researchers showed people a TV show, some with ad breaks and some without, and the people whose show was interrupted by ads enjoyed it more, even though they said they'd rather skip the ads. The break stopped them from getting used to it. So the people who queued in 2007 weren't wrong. The thrill was real, it just faded. Next time your favourite thing starts to feel boring, don't replace it. Take a break from it, and let it feel new again.
