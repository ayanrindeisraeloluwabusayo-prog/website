# Why talking to yourself is actually smart (Full Blend)

You're looking for your keys, and you hear yourself muttering: keys, keys, where are my keys? Or right before something scary, you whisper: come on, you can do this. And then you wonder if that's a little bit crazy. Remember when Malala Yousafzai sat down with Jon Stewart in 2013? She told him that when she was afraid the Taliban would come for her, she would ask herself: if he comes, what would you do, Malala? She was talking to herself, by name. Now, scientists have tested exactly this, and in 2012 two psychologists, Gary Lupyan and Daniel Swingley, asked people to find objects in pictures, and you see, when they said the object's name out loud, they found it faster. So muttering keys, keys is actually helping your brain search. But here's the twist: how you talk to yourself matters. In 2014, psychologist Ethan Kross gave people five minutes to prepare a speech about why they deserved their dream job. The ones who coached themselves using their own name, or you, instead of I, felt more confident, gave better speeches, and brooded less afterwards. And in 2017, his team found it calms the brain's emotional reaction within about one second. So Malala wasn't losing it. She was doing exactly what the science says. Next time you're nervous, don't say I can do this. Say your own name. You've got this.
