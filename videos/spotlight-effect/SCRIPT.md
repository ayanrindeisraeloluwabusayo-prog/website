# Why you think everybody noticed your mistake: the spotlight effect (Full Blend, v1, approved)

You trip on the stairs at a party. Your face goes hot. You're sure the whole room saw it, and they'll remember it all night. Remember when you walked into a room and felt like every eye turned to you? In 2000, psychologists at Cornell University tested that feeling. They made students put on an embarrassing T-shirt with a cheesy pop singer's face on it, then walk into a room full of other students. Afterwards, the students in the shirt guessed that about half the room had noticed it. And you see, when the researchers asked the room, fewer than a quarter could say what was on the shirt. People expected twice as many eyes as there really were. Psychologists called it the spotlight effect: you feel like you're standing under a spotlight, because you're the one person you see most clearly. You know every detail of your own mistake, so you think everyone else does too. But here's the twist: they ran it again with a shirt the students were proud to wear, and the result was the same. Good or bad, we think people notice us far more than they do. We're each the centre of our own world, and everyone else is busy standing under their own spotlight. So the next time you mess up in public, remember: most people didn't see it, and the ones who did have already gone back to worrying about themselves.
