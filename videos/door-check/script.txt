You lock the door. You walk away. And three steps later, a voice in your head says: did you really lock it? So you go back. It's locked. You walk away again. And somehow, you feel even less sure than before. Now, in 2003, two Dutch psychologists, Marcel van den Hout and Merel Kindt, wanted to know why, so they sat ordinary people in front of a gas stove on a computer screen and asked them to turn it off and check it, again and again, twenty times. And you see, when they tested them afterwards, their memory was just as accurate as everyone else's, they still knew the stove was off. But here's the twist: they no longer believed it, because the memory of that last check had gone blurry, with less detail and less colour, so they trusted it less. And three years later, another team tried it with a real kitchen stove, and the same thing happened. So the more you check, the less you trust your own memory. Next time, check once. Look at it properly. Then walk away, because going back won't make you surer. It will make you less sure.
