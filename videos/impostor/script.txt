Now, that feeling got its name back in 1978, when two psychologists, Pauline Clance and Suzanne Imes, kept meeting the same kind of woman in therapy and in their classes, more than a hundred and fifty of them, women with top grades and top jobs, and every one was quietly sure she had fooled everybody. And you see, whenever one of them succeeded, she blamed it on luck, or hard work, or charm, anything except actually being good, so they called it the impostor phenomenon. But here's the twist: they thought it was mostly women, and then in 2020, a review of sixty-two studies found that men feel it just as much.
