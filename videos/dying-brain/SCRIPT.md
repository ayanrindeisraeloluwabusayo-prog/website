# What happens in your brain when you die (Full Blend, v1, waiting for approval)

Your heart stops. Your breathing stops. And then, what does your brain do? Most of us imagine it simply switches off, like a light. Remember when Sharon Stone said that after a bleed in her brain in 2001, she saw a giant vortex of white light, and felt pulled toward it? And you see, she's not alone. In a 2001 study in the Netherlands, of 344 people brought back after their hearts stopped, about one in five, 18 percent, described a near-death experience. Now, for years scientists thought the dying brain just goes quiet. So in 2023, Jimo Borjigin and her team at the University of Michigan recorded the brain waves of four patients as their life support was switched off. But here's the twist: in two of them, the brain didn't fade. It lit up. Fast brain waves called gamma waves, linked to awareness and memory, surged, especially in areas tied to seeing and dreaming. We don't know if they experienced anything. But the brain was busier than anyone expected. And brain cells don't all die at once. In 2019, scientists at Yale pumped a special fluid through pig brains four hours after death, and some cells started working again, though there was no sign of awareness. So the end may not be a light switch. It may be one last burst of activity. Next time someone tells you about a tunnel of light, don't laugh. Their brain may have been working harder than ever.

## Sources (checked)
- Sharon Stone: 2001 brain bleed (ruptured vertebral artery); has described "a giant vortex of white light" and being pulled outward and upward (interviews; memoir The Beauty of Living Twice, 2021).
- van Lommel et al., "Near-death experience in survivors of cardiac arrest: a prospective study in the Netherlands", The Lancet 358:2039-45 (2001): 344 resuscitated patients in 10 Dutch hospitals; 62 (18%) reported a near-death experience.
- Xu, Borjigin et al., "Surge of neurophysiological coupling and connectivity of gamma oscillations in the dying human brain", PNAS (May 2023), University of Michigan: 4 comatose patients, EEG as ventilator support was withdrawn; 2 showed a gamma surge, strongest in the temporo-parietal-occipital "hot zone" linked to dreaming, visual experience and consciousness. Small study; no evidence the patients experienced anything.
- Vrselja et al., "Restoration of brain circulation and cellular functions hours post-mortem", Nature (April 2019), Yale: BrainEx perfusion of 32 pig brains starting 4 hours after death restored some cellular functions; no organized electrical activity of awareness or consciousness.
