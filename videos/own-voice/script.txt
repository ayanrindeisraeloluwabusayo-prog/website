Why do you hate your own voice on a voice note? You press play, and a stranger answers. Higher. Thinner. Not you. But it is you. Here's what's happening. When you speak, the sound reaches your ears two ways. Through the air, like everyone else hears it. And through the bones of your skull. Bone carries the deep notes better. So inside your head, your voice sounds deeper and richer than it really is. A recording only catches the air. The bass is gone. That shock is so common, psychologists gave it a name back in 1966. Voice confrontation. And here's the twist. In a 2013 study, people heard their own voice without knowing it was theirs. They rated it more attractive than anyone else did. So the voice you hate, is the only one your friends have ever heard. And they like it just fine.
