# Why nobody asks questions in class (pluralistic ignorance)

Script style: **Full Blend** (You First -> Star Proof -> Story Flow -> close on the star, then the viewer).
The "star" here is a famous story everyone knows, not a celebrity. Draft for the owner's approval.

## Script (~230 words, about 72 s)

The teacher finishes, looks at the class and asks: any questions?
You're completely lost. But you look around, and everyone looks calm. So you stay quiet. And so does everyone else.

Now listen to this. You already know this story. In 1837, Hans Christian Andersen wrote about an emperor who walked
through town in clothes that didn't exist. Every adult could see he was naked, and every one of them stayed silent,
because they thought everyone else could see the clothes. Until one child said it out loud.

Now, psychologists have a name for this, pluralistic ignorance, and in 1968 two of them, Bibb Latane and John Darley,
showed how far it goes, when they sat students in a room to fill in a form and then slowly pumped smoke through a vent
in the wall. And you see, when a student was alone, three out of four got up and reported it, but when they sat with
two strangers who had secretly been told to ignore it, only one in ten said anything, and the rest kept writing in a
room filling with smoke, because nobody else looked worried.

But here's the twist: the calm faces around you are usually hiding the same doubt. When Princeton students were asked
about heavy drinking on campus, most were less comfortable with it than they thought everyone else was.

So the emperor walked naked because nobody wanted to go first. Next time the teacher asks for questions, ask yours.
Half the room is waiting for someone to go first.

## Facts and where each comes from
- Hans Christian Andersen, "The Emperor's New Clothes", published 1837 (public domain story).
- Latane, B., & Darley, J. M. (1968). Group inhibition of bystander intervention in emergencies. Journal of
  Personality and Social Psychology, 10(3), 215-221. Smoke through a wall vent while students filled in a form: about
  75 % alone reported it; with two passive confederates, about 10 %.
- Prentice, D. A., & Miller, D. T. (1993). Pluralistic ignorance and alcohol use on campus. Journal of Personality and
  Social Psychology, 64(2), 243-256. Princeton students rated their own comfort with campus drinking as lower than the
  average student's.
- The classroom opening is an everyday scene, not presented as a study.
