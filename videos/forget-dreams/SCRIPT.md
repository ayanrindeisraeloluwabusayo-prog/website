# Why we forget our dreams (Full Blend, v1, approved)

You wake up in the middle of an amazing dream. You can still see it. Then you check your phone, and it's gone. Remember when you tried to tell someone your dream at breakfast, and it just fell apart? And you see, the sleeping brain is bad at saving new memories. To keep a dream, scientists think you need to wake up, even briefly, while it's still fresh. In 2013, researchers in Lyon, France, compared people who remember their dreams almost every morning with people who remember about two a month. The high recallers woke up during the night about twice as often, and their brains reacted more to sounds while they slept. Every little wake-up was a chance to save the dream. But here's the twist: forgetting may be on purpose. In 2019, scientists in Japan and the US found a group of brain cells in mice that are most active during dream sleep, and their job seems to be forgetting. When the scientists switched those cells off during dream sleep, the mice remembered more. So while you sleep, your brain may be clearing out the clutter of the day, and your dreams get swept out with it. If you want to keep one, don't move, don't check your phone. Say it out loud or write it down the moment you wake up.

## Sources (checked)
- Eichenlaub, Bertrand, Morlet & Ruby, "Brain reactivity differentiates subjects with high and low dream recall frequencies during both sleep and wakefulness", Cerebral Cortex (2013/2014), Inserm / Lyon Neuroscience Research Center: 41 people; high recallers about 5.2 mornings a week vs about 2 dreams a month; high recallers had about twice as much wakefulness during sleep and stronger brain responses to sounds. Ruby: the sleeping brain cannot memorise new information; it needs to awaken to do that.
- Izawa, Yamanaka, Kilduff et al., "REM sleep-active MCH neurons are involved in forgetting hippocampus-dependent memories", Science 365:1308-1313 (2019), Nagoya University and SRI International (NIH-funded): MCH neurons in the mouse hypothalamus, most active in REM sleep, help forget; silencing them during REM improved memory. A mouse study; the link to human dreams is the researchers' suggestion.
- The tip at the end (say or write it straight away) is common practical advice, not a study finding.
