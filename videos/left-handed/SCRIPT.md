# Why are some people left-handed? (Full Blend, v1, approved; thumbnail C)

Pick up a pen. Which hand did you use? For about nine in every ten people, it's the right. Remember when Barack Obama, in his first days as president in 2009, signed papers with his left hand and joked, "That's right. I'm a lefty. Get used to it"? And you see, he's in good company. In 2020, a review of studies covering more than two million people found that about ten and a half percent of us are left-handed. So is it in the genes? Partly. Studies of tens of thousands of twins found that genes explain only about a quarter of it. There's no single lefty gene. Dozens of tiny genetic differences each nudge it a little. But here's the twist: your hand may be chosen before you're born. In Belfast, psychologist Peter Hepper used ultrasound to watch babies in the womb sucking their thumbs. Years later, he checked back with seventy-five of them, now aged ten to twelve. Every one of the sixty who had sucked their right thumb was right-handed. Most who sucked their left were left-handed. So being left-handed isn't a mistake, and it isn't just from your parents. It's a mix of genes and chance that starts in the womb. Next time someone calls lefties odd, remind them: one in ten people, and at least one president, would disagree.

- Barack Obama, early 2009: "That's right. I'm a lefty. Get used to it." (ABC News, "Obama Latest of Presidential Southpaws"; Mother Jones quote of the day, 20 Jan 2009).
- Papadatou-Pastou et al., "Human handedness: A meta-analysis", Psychological Bulletin 146(6) (2020): 2,396,170 people; best estimate of left-handedness 10.6%.
- Medland et al. (2006, 35 twin samples, 21,000+ pairs; 2009, 50,000+ twins and siblings): additive genetic effects explain about 25% of the variance in handedness. Cuellar-Partida et al. (2021) GWAS: 48 variants, highly polygenic.
- Hepper, Shahidullah & White (1991), Neuropsychologia; Hepper, Wells & Lynch (2005), Neuropsychologia, Queen's University Belfast: fetal thumb-sucking seen on ultrasound; at 10-12 years, all 60 right-thumb suckers were right-handed; 10 of 15 left-thumb suckers were left-handed.
