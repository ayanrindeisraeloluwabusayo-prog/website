# Mood brief: "Why a song gives you chills" (30 s)

One picture per scene (sometimes two in quick succession). Each line says **what the words say**,
**what it should feel like**, and **what kind of picture carries that feeling**. Text colour follows the
owner's rule: white on pictures and blue, black on white/paper, blue only as an accent.

| # | Time | Words on screen | Feeling | Picture idea |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0–3.4 s | That **chill** down your spine | The hook. A held breath, a shiver, private and physical. Slow, close, a little dark. | A face or sculpted head turned up with eyes closed, as if lost in sound; or a close-up of skin, a neck, a back. Moody black and white or warm amber. |
| 2 | 3.4–6.8 s | isn't magic. It's **chemistry.** | A crisp turn from feeling to explanation. Curious, a bit playful, "here's the trick behind it". | Old alchemy or chemistry engravings, glassware, a lab lit by one glow. Reads well as blue duotone. |
| 3 | 6.8–10.4 s | When a song you love builds toward its **peak,** | Rising tension. Something gathering and climbing, almost there. | A huge wave about to break, a storm building, a climber near the top, an orchestra in full swell. |
| 4 | 10.4–14.0 s | your brain releases **dopamine** | Warm release. A glow spreading, light pouring in. The happiest scene. | Fireworks, bursts of light, glowing gas clouds in space, or a beautiful old drawing of brain cells. |
| 5 | 14.0–17.5 s | **before** the drop even lands. | Suspense, a moment frozen in mid-air. The surprise of the video. | A figure mid-fall or mid-leap, a wave at its crest, a diver in the air. Push in hard. |
| 6 | 17.5–22.5 s | In 2011, researchers **scanned** listeners' brains. | Evidence. Calm, clinical, credible, a little "lab". Proof, not drama. | Old anatomy plates of the brain, a brain scan, a book page; then the study page with the yellow highlighter. |
| 7 | 22.5–26.5 s | **Anticipation** lit up one region. The **peak,** lit up another. | Two lights switching on in the dark, one after the other. Clear and almost diagrammatic. | City lights seen from space, two stars or galaxies, two lamps in the night, drawings of brain cells. |
| 8 | 26.5–28.9 s | The build-up isn't the wait. It's the **reward.** | Resolution, standing at the top after the climb. Wide, open, satisfied. | A lone figure looking out over a vast view, a sunrise, light breaking over a horizon. |
| 9 | 28.9–30.0 s | Follow for more **music science** | The end card. Confident, designed, on blue. | A blue card (no photo needed); optionally a small classical bust or a musician as a cut-out. |

## Rules for choosing pictures
- Public domain first (Art Institute of Chicago, NASA, old works on Wikimedia Commons). Any CC BY or
  BY-SA picture needs its credit line in the video description.
- No famous film clips, no logos, no recognisable living people.
- Every picture gets a slow drift or push-in, grain, and a moody grade so white text reads on it.
- Leave empty space in the frame where the words go.
