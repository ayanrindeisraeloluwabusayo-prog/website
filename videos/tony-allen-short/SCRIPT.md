## Facts (each line of the script rests on one of these)
1. Tony Allen was born in Lagos in 1940 and died in Paris on 30 April 2020, aged 79.
   ([Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/tony-allen-afrobeat-drummer-fela-kuti-dead-992588/), [The Current](https://www.thecurrent.org/feature/2020/04/30/tony-allen-afrobeats-foundational-drummer-has-died-at-age-79))
2. He did not take up drums until about 18 and taught himself, studying LPs and magazine tutorials by Gene
   Krupa, Art Blakey and Max Roach. ([Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/feature/tony-allen-drummer-afrobeat-obituary), [Pan African Music](https://pan-african-music.com/en/tony-allen-death/), [Modern Drummer](https://www.moderndrummer.com/2011/10/tony-allen-afrobeat-innovator/))
3. Victor Olaiya hired him to play claves in his highlife band, the Cool Cats; when the band's drummer left,
   Allen took the drum chair. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Allen_(musician)), [Eurasia Review interview](https://www.eurasiareview.com/03052020-remembering-legendary-nigerian-drummer-tony-allen-interview/))
4. He met Fela Kuti in 1964 and was drummer and musical director of Africa '70 (1968-1979); together they created
   Afrobeat. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Allen_(musician)), [Guardian obituary via Yahoo](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/tony-allen-drummer-fela-kuti-155034363.html))
5. Fela said "without Tony Allen, there would be no Afrobeat", and told him he sounded like four drummers at once.
   ([Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/feature/tony-allen-drummer-afrobeat-obituary), [All About Jazz](https://www.allaboutjazz.com/musicians/tony-allen/))
6. Brian Eno called him "perhaps the greatest drummer who ever lived". ([KCRW](https://www.kcrw.com/stories/tony-allen-obituary-afrobeat-drummer-fela), [Guardian, May 2020](https://moredarkthanshark.org/eno_int_guardian-may20.html))

Visual cause and effect: four limbs, each playing its own rhythm, add up to the sound of four drummers.
Everyday analogy: patting your head while rubbing your tummy, then adding both feet.

## Script (about 200 words, ~55 s at 3.7 words/s)
| Beat | Voice (Brian) | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | One boy from Lagos learned to sound like **four drummers at once**, with just two hands and two feet. | A small boy behind a drum kit; four ghost copies of him appear around the kit, then snap back into one. |
| Setting | Lagos, the late 1950s. Tony Allen doesn't touch a drum until he's **eighteen**. | Golden Lagos street: yellow buses, market stalls, a lagoon. Tony walks past a band, listening. |
| Action 1 | He teaches himself, from jazz records and magazine lessons by American drummers. | His room at night: a record spinning, a magazine open, Tony tapping along on his knees. |
| Action 2 | His first job in a real band? Not drums. **Claves**: two little wooden sticks. Click. Click. | A highlife bandstand; Tony at the back with two tiny sticks, very serious. Click, click. |
| Snap | Then the band's drummer **quits**. | The drummer storms off; an empty drum stool, spotlight. |
| Deadpan double | Luckily, Tony was standing right there. Unluckily for every other drummer in Lagos, he was **ready**. | Tony looks at the stool. Cut: he sits. Rival drummers' faces drop. |
| Explanation | His secret is **independence**. Try patting your head while rubbing your tummy. Now add both feet, each with its own rhythm. Four limbs, four patterns, one man who sounds like a whole drum section. | A kid tries the head-and-tummy trick and fails; Tony does it, then four coloured rhythm lines flow from his four limbs into one groove. |
| Consequence | In 1964 he meets a bandleader called **Fela Kuti**. Together they invent a brand new sound: **Afrobeat**. Fela later said: without Tony Allen, there would be no Afrobeat. | Fela at the front with a saxophone, Tony behind the kit; the crowd starts moving; the word-free sign of a packed club at night. |
| Callback twist | The boy who started with two little sticks ended up called, by Brian Eno, perhaps the **greatest drummer who ever lived**. | Back to the two tiny claves, now on a shelf beside a glowing drum kit; final wide of Lagos at golden hour. |
