# The owner's story playbook for cartoon shorts

Read this before writing any script for a cartoon short. It combines the owner's own breakdown of five
reference shorts with a second-by-second study of their narration and pictures (10 Nov 2026). Technique only:
never reuse those shorts' stories, lines, characters or shots, and never name the channel.

The first short made here (Tony Allen) was a biography: facts in time order, no doubter, no danger, the
explanation in the middle, a compliment for an ending. The owner found it less gripping. Do not write that again.

## The six beats (the owner's table)
| # | Beat | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A weird action | 0-3 s | Someone does something that looks stupid. The "why" is held back. No name, date or intro. |
| 2 | Stakes | 3-12 s | What is at risk, in one line. An early "You see," may open this setup. |
| 3 | A doubter mocks them | 12-25 s | A boss figure says what the viewer is thinking, in a quoted line. That line is the joke. |
| 4 | They lose at first | 25-40 s | The doubter looks right, the rival wins, the hero is laughed at harder. |
| 5 | "You see..." | about 70 % | A short explanation with a homely comparison (soup, a coin, a clay pot). |
| 6 | Payoff, then stop | last 5 s | One picture of justice that echoes the first line. No outro, no "follow me". |

Second format: the same beats with "you" as the main character ("Imagine your boss walks you to a tiny room...").
Use it for everyday topics with no historical hero.

## Do
- Story first, fact second: the fact is about 15 % of the runtime and lands because the viewer wants the underdog to win.
- Present tense, short sentences, spoken dialogue for the doubter.
- Concrete, small, rising details: "39 feet", "a coin... then a sheet of paper", "one ball, then another, then all eight".
- Measured pace of the references: 215-245 words for 60-65 s (3.3-3.8 words a second). A 40 s short is about 145 words.
- End on the payoff word. The last line should call back to the first.
- Nigerian everyday settings (garri, NEPA and the generator, harmattan, tomatoes going bad, mosquitoes, the talking
  drum, family pressure, market day), with science or history anyone in the world can follow.
- Keep the core fact true and sourced. Tell the owner which scenes and lines are dramatised, and write those as story
  ("the story goes", unnamed characters), never as claimed history. Real named people in serious events (for example
  Dr Adadevoh) get no invented mockery or dialogue.

## Don't
- No biography or timeline structure, no opening with a name, a year or "Did you know".
- No explanation before the tension has peaked.
- No outro, moral speech, or call to follow.
- No vague wording ("a long time", "very big") where a number or an object would do.
- No invented facts, figures or quotes presented as real. Unchecked numbers are flagged to the owner.
- No American-default settings when a Nigerian one tells the same idea.

## Look (why the references read as a real place and ours read as "too cartoon")
- Characters stay simple; the world is treated like a film set. Give sets texture and many props; avoid plain
  coloured boxes and empty ground.
- One colour mood per scene from real lighting: low sun behind the subject, moonlit blue nights, glowing lamps, haze.
- One set shown from ten or more angles: low, top-down, over the shoulder, wide establishing, close-ups on objects.
  A new angle every 1-2 s, with slow push-ins.
- Depth: blurred backgrounds, foreground objects.
- Explanations happen inside the world (a cut-open object in the same scene), never on a flat graphics screen.
- Captions: 1-2 plain white bold words with a dark outline, centred low-middle. No pill, no colour highlight.
- Honest limit: the references use detailed modelled assets; sets here are built in code, so the gap can be
  narrowed a lot but may not close fully. Say so.

## Story bank (fact-check each before scripting)
Garri and cyanide (why cassava must wait) · the pot-in-pot fridge (Mohammed Bah Abba, Jigawa) · the drum that
speaks Yoruba · harmattan dust feeding the Amazon · Dr Ameyo Adadevoh and Ebola, 2014 (respectful, no invented
drama) · why mosquitoes choose you · the Dufuna canoe (Yobe) · why doors shrink in harmattan · palm wine getting
stronger by evening · the Kano dye pits.
